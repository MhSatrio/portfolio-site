RevOps Operating System, Template Guide
A practical Revenue Operations workbook for Property, Hospitality (Hotel & Restaurant), EXIM/Trading, and B2B SaaS companies.
1. Purpose
This workbook is a plug-and-play RevOps operating system: enter operational data (leads, pipeline, revenue, customers, cash) and it automatically calculates KPIs, targets vs. actuals, funnel conversion, forecasts, revenue leakage, and the actions management should take next. It replaces a scattered mix of KPI decks with one live, auditable model.
2. Who should use this
Founders, GMs, RevOps/Sales Ops managers, and finance leads at small-to-medium companies who want one shared source of truth for revenue performance across sales, marketing, and customer success, without building custom BI.
3. What "RevOps" means here
RevOps (Revenue Operations) aligns marketing, sales, customer success and finance around one funnel and one set of numbers. In this workbook that means: every KPI on the Dashboard traces back to a raw data table, every target is centralized and editable in Settings, and nothing is hardcoded into a formula.
4. Industries covered
Property Residential, villa, luxury, commercial & land brokerage/agency, 13_Property
Hospitality, Hotel Rooms revenue, occupancy, ADR, RevPAR, channel mix, 14_Hospitality_Hotel
Hospitality, Restaurant Covers, average check, table turnover, RevPASH, 15_Hospitality_Restaurant
EXIM / Import-Export / Trading Supplier→landed cost→sale, inventory, cash cycle, 16_EXIM
Technology / B2B SaaS MRR/ARR, expansion & churn, NRR/GRR, CAC:LTV, 17_Tech_SaaS
5. The universal RevOps flow
Leads / Demand → Qualified Leads → Opportunities → Proposal / Quote → Won → Revenue → Gross Profit → Cash → Retention / Repeat Revenue
Every universal tab (03–12) sits on this flow. Every industry tab (13–17) adapts the middle of the flow to how that industry actually sells (e.g. Property: Listing→Enquiry→Viewing→Offer→Closed; SaaS: Trial→Opportunity→Subscription→Renewal→Expansion).
6. How to use this workbook
1. Configure Settings, company, currency, period, and every target/benchmark.
2. Enter Leads (03), every inbound/outbound lead, one row each.
3. Enter Opportunities (04_Pipeline), once a lead is qualified, log the deal.
4. Enter Revenue (07) and Cash (08), actual transactions and payments.
5. Enter Customer data (06) and the relevant industry tab (13–17).
6. Review the Dashboard (20), weekly/monthly executive snapshot.
7. Review Revenue Leakage (18), where money is being lost, and how much.
8. Work the Action Tracker (19), every metric below target surfaces here automatically.
7. Which tabs you fill in manually vs. what's automatic
MANUAL ENTRY (blue-shaded cells): 02_Settings, 03_Leads, 04_Pipeline, 05_Activities, 06_Customers, 07_Revenue, 08_Cash, 13–17 industry tabs. AUTOMATIC (grey-shaded formula cells): every KPI Summary box, 09_Forecast, 10_Channel, 11_Salesperson, 12_Cohort, 18_Revenue_Leakage, 19_Action_Tracker, 20_Dashboard. TARGETS/ASSUMPTIONS (yellow-shaded cells): 02_Settings only, every formula elsewhere references these cells, never a typed-in number.
8. Universal vs. industry-specific metrics
Universal metrics (apply to all five industries): lead volume, qualification rate, win rate, average deal size, sales cycle, pipeline coverage, revenue, gross margin, DSO, retention/churn, forecast accuracy. Industry-specific metrics (only meaningful in one vertical) live only on that industry's tab, e.g. RevPAR is meaningless for EXIM, and Landed Cost is meaningless for a hotel. See 01_Glossary column "Applies To" for every metric's scope.
9. Customizing targets
Go to 02_Settings. Every target/benchmark is laid out as Warning → Target → Excellent. Change the yellow cells only, every formula in the workbook references these by name, so nothing breaks. Reference benchmarks (industry norms sourced externally) are explicitly labeled "Reference Benchmark", treat them as a starting point, not a guarantee, since they vary by geography, segment and business model.
10. Recommended data-entry frequency
Leads & Activities: daily. Pipeline: whenever a deal moves stage (at least weekly). Revenue & Cash: as invoices/payments occur, reconciled weekly. Customers: on acquisition, churn, or expansion events. Industry tabs: match the transaction cycle (e.g. Restaurant = daily service; Property = per listing/viewing/offer).
11. Recommended review cadence
WEEKLY: Dashboard + Pipeline + Action Tracker (15 min standing review). MONTHLY: full Dashboard, Revenue Leakage, Cohort, Forecast vs. Actual, Salesperson performance. QUARTERLY: re-baseline Settings targets, review benchmark relevance, deep-dive the largest leakage category.
12. Dashboard traffic-light status
Green, Excellent Metric meets or beats the Excellent threshold in Settings.
Yellow, Warning Metric is between Warning and Target, needs attention, not yet critical.
Red, Critical Metric is below the Warning threshold, needs immediate action.
Grey/Blue, N/A or informational Not enough data yet to calculate, or the cell is purely informational.
13. Revenue leakage analysis
18_Revenue_Leakage compares Potential Value vs. Actual Value at every stage of the funnel (lead, qualification, proposal, close, discount, churn, cancellation, collection, inventory, margin) and quantifies the gap. It answers: where are we losing revenue, how much is it worth, and what should we fix first? Every leakage row that has enough data gets a dollar figure; rows without enough input data show N/A rather than a guess.
14. Forecast methodology
09_Forecast is probability-weighted, not guaranteed revenue. Best Case = all open pipeline weighted at each stage's probability, uncapped. Base Case = weighted pipeline only for deals with an expected close date in the period, plus closed revenue. Worst Case = closed revenue plus only pipeline in Negotiation/Proposal stages. All three are clearly labeled as scenarios, treat Base Case as the planning number and Best/Worst as the range.
